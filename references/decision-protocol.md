# Independent decision protocol

Use this protocol when the user proposes buying, selling, adding, reducing, switching, timing, or ranking an investment. It also applies when the phrasing assumes action, such as “which one is best?”

## 1. Reframe the decision

Identify the actual choice set. Always include:

- the proposed action;
- a credible alternative exposure or implementation;
- continuing the existing plan;
- waiting or taking no action.

Do not begin product comparison until establishing whether the exposure itself is justified.

## 2. Form and lock an independent stance

Using evidence available at the cutoff, form a preliminary direction (upward bias, downward bias, range or unresolved) and assess the current action (build, add, hold, reduce, exit or wait), before adapting to the user's preference. Record the main reason, confidence, horizon and what would change it. Do not upgrade confidence because the user repeats the preference or already holds the asset.

The legacy `reject for now`, `watch`, `worth dedicated research` and `review threshold reached` labels remain usable for collection priority and research progress. They are not the final investment stance and do not decide the action by themselves. Complete the material follow-up within the request, then choose the preferred action from the evidence.

Do not offer a token small-buy solely to appease the user. A staged first entry is valid when it has its own thesis, entry economics and invalidation, even if some technical confirmation is still pending. Without portfolio inputs, do not invent personal amounts or total-asset weights; a labeled plan-budget scenario may express proportions.

## 3. Build the evidence dossier

For the proposal and its best alternative, establish:

- underlying economic or fundamental thesis;
- what changed and why now;
- valuation or price context where reliable;
- catalyst and expected horizon;
- market, liquidity, currency, policy, implementation, and product risks;
- evidence quality and material gaps;
- confirmation and invalidation conditions.

Separate the asset thesis from the product wrapper. A strong underlying thesis does not make every fund or share class attractive.

## 4. Run the adversarial review

Use three functions, whether performed by one model or several:

Multiple roles using the same model and evidence are not independent confirmation. Treat their agreement as structured synthesis; raise confidence only when distinct evidence supports it.

### Advocate

Present the strongest evidence-based case for the proposal. Address the most serious objection rather than listing generic positives.

### Challenger

Try to disprove the proposal. Identify the strongest contrary evidence, a plausible competing explanation, hidden assumptions, opportunity cost, and conditions under which waiting wins.

### Risk manager

Judge portfolio interaction, downside path, liquidity, implementation frictions, correlation shifts, and whether the evidence is strong enough to justify action. The risk manager may veto an otherwise attractive thesis when the portfolio or evidence cannot support it.

Do not reward rhetorical confidence. The winning case is the one best supported by timely, comparable evidence.

The risk review distinguishes facts that invalidate the thesis from ordinary uncertainty that can be priced or sized. It does not veto action merely because future success is unproved or an attractive setup lacks every technical confirmation. For catalyst anticipation, state what is already known, what is expected, what the price seems to discount and what happens if the event disappoints; do not relabel an expected policy as implemented.

## 5. Compare decisions, not stories

Use a compact comparison:

| Choice | Eligible now? | Expected role | Supporting evidence | Main failure mode | Confirmation needed | Cost of waiting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Avoid unsupported target prices or probabilities. When information is insufficient, specify the missing observation rather than forcing a ranking.

Make every decision-relevant condition operational in one trigger register:

| Type | Metric or event | Source | Direction or threshold | Observation window | Next review point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| confirmation / review / invalidation |  |  |  |  |  |

Every cell is required. Use a numeric threshold only when the evidence supplies a defensible baseline. Otherwise use a specific event rule, such as two consecutive named releases or a funded policy disbursement. If a rule cannot yet be defined, label it `not operational`; it cannot support an action or candidate promotion. Do not leave words such as `small`, `meaningful`, `continued`, `stabilized`, `improved`, or `weakened` undefined.

Distinguish a directly measurable action condition from a source event that starts further analyst review. For “holds above a moving average”, specify the exact average, number of completed closes, each day's updated comparison and when the window begins. For a rate-change condition, name the already-public entry baseline and whether any observation or only the window endpoint triggers it. Compare portfolio changes at the same denominator and disclosed look-through scope; an unresolved child is unknown, not zero. Do not invent a numerical “significance” threshold merely to automate judgment: name the report/event to review and leave the trading action pending that review.

## 6. Personalize last

Only after the independent stance is locked, load authoritative holdings and assess:

- overlap and concentration;
- geographic, sector, factor, duration, commodity, and currency exposure;
- liquidity needs and recurring-investment interaction;
- product fees, tracking, limits, settlement, valuation lag, and tax or transaction frictions when relevant.

If authoritative amounts or weights are missing, do not invent personalized position sizes.

## 7. Reach the final action decision

The exposure stance is a preliminary research state. After completing its required evidence and review, decide whether to build, add, hold, reduce, exit or wait. Give the investment judgment and the observable entry/addition conditions, relevant price zone or event window, invalidation and review point. Do not stop at a due-diligence label when the user asked for a final decision and the evidence supports one.

A personal size needs the user's risk budget, current position and available capital. When those are absent, keep the independent market decision and supply clearly labeled conditional budget scenarios or the sizing inputs needed. Do not fabricate actual portfolio percentages or cash. Explain whether timing is an analytical judgment or a historically tested strategy, and use calibrated probabilities only when their validation exists.

## 8. Deliver a direct conclusion

Lead with whether action is justified. If disagreeing with the user, say `I disagree with this action at present` or an equally direct formulation, followed by evidence and what could change the view.

Give the preferred action and horizon early, followed by the decisive evidence, strongest countercase and practical conditions. End with the next decision/review point. Include an unresolved gap only if it changes the chosen action, size, share class or timing; explain that effect once. Engineering/test limitations belong in validation records rather than routine research prose. Do not end a weak thesis with a token action, or a well-supported thesis with generic reasons to postpone all decisions.
